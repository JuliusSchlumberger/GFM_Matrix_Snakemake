Headline findings

What the 40-tile sample changes about the pilot's story

The 7-tile pilot suggested a fairly clean picture: most tiles converge quickly, a few large ones need more sweeps, and the outer loop's value scales roughly with tile size. At 40 tiles the size correlation mostly disappears — convergence behaviour turns out to depend more on a tile's coastline/terrain complexity than its pixel count, and the caps tightened for this run (24 sweeps, 3 outer iterations) are demonstrably too tight for a meaningful minority of tiles.

  • !
    9 of 40 tiles (22%) never reach a sustained plateau within the 24-sweep ceiling used for this run — residual change keeps trickling in, sometimes past sweep 20. Production's max_rounds=12 (48 sweeps) has real headroom over this, but this run can't directly confirm those 9 settle by sweep 48 — it never ran them that far.
  • !
    15 of 40 tiles (38%) show a single-sweep jump over 5% in flooded-cell count somewhere in the trace, well after an earlier, misleadingly quiet period — the pilot's "false plateau" finding isn't a rare edge case, it's closer to a third of real tiles.
  • O
    The outer loop's marginal value over the free static filter is not size-correlated at this scale — it's scattered across small, medium, and large tiles alike. In 9 of 38 tiles the outer loop actually contributes more than the static filter did.
  • !
    7 of 38 tiles hit the 3-outer-iteration cap used for this run without reaching their own convergence criterion — a real signal that the tightened cap (vs. production's 5) is under-provisioned for some tiles, not just a study-speed shortcut.

Caveat on sample coverage

This 40-tile sample tops out at ~52M cells — the wet-tile search stopped once 40 were found, so the largest candidates in the pool (up to ~207M cells, p90–p99.5) were never run here. The pilot's own largest tile (497, ~207M cells, sweep 2→3 jump of 10.6%/15.0%) remains the only real data point at that scale. Nothing here contradicts it, but nothing here re-confirms it either.

Found along the way

Real bugs, not just calibration data

Running this at 40-tile scale (vs. the pilot's 7) surfaced production issues the smaller sample never touched.

Friction silently decoded to float64, not float32

A numpy legacy-casting quirk (float32_array / python_int(1_000_000) upcasts) meant decode_friction_int16 returned float64 despite its own docstring. Since solve_eikonal_dense derives its dominant array's dtype from friction, every production flood solve — not just this study — was running at double the memory it needed. Fixed by dividing by an explicit np.float32 scale.

Mask cast to int64 for no functional reason

Every use of the mask array is a plain equality comparison — dtype-agnostic. int64 was 8x more memory than the handful of small codes it holds ever needed. Fixed to int8.

DEM nodata leak on zero-coverage tiles

When a tile's entire read window has no valid DeltaDTM elevation at all (rare, but real at small/edge tiles), the small-gap interpolation step had nothing to interpolate from and silently left the raw −9999 nodata sentinel in place instead of hard-filling it. Traced via a real crash on a ~3K-pixel tile; fixed with a fallback to the existing 99m "never floods" sentinel.

Calibration script crashed on zero-station tiles

Not a production bug — this study's own script didn't handle the "no COAST-RP station found" case the way production does, and crashed building a nearest-neighbour tree from zero points. Fixed to match production's own empty-boundaries handling.

Open questions

What this data doesn't settle yet

Does production's max_rounds=12 (48 sweeps) actually cover the 9 tiles that didn't settle by sweep 24 here?
Likely, based on the pilot's own large tiles fully settling by sweep 48 — but this run didn't go past 24, so it's inference from a different, smaller sample, not direct confirmation.
Is max_outer_iterations=5 (production) enough for the 7 tiles that hit this run's 3-iteration cap?
Unknown from this data — would need a follow-up run at the production cap specifically on those 7 tiles to confirm they converge rather than just taking longer to hit the same wall.
What actually drives the "late jump" and "outer-dominant" behaviour?
Neither correlates cleanly with tile size in this sample. Coastline complexity / number of distinct inlets is the leading hypothesis but untested here — would need geometry data per tile to check.
