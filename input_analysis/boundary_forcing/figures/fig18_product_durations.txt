Peak durations of the two hydrograph products at the 22,670 COAST-RP stations north of 60 S. T(f) =
hours the hydrograph stays within f x peak of its peak (hourly +-72 h profile normalised by the
peak, relative to local MSL; as in the acceptance test of fig10). COAST-HG has one RP100 hydrograph
per station, which production uses for every RP; the level product has one per RP. (a, b) Median
T(0.25) and T(0.5) by spring tidal range. (c) T(0.25) at RP100, product over COAST-HG; orange = the
product's peak is wider.

Reading: the product's peaks are wider than COAST-HG's: median T(0.25) 12 h against 6 h at RP100 and
T(0.5) 36 h against 23 h. T falls with RP (T(0.25) 13 h at RP2, 11 h at RP1000), as the gauges show
(fig10), which a single COAST-HG shape cannot. The duration correction B reads T from these
hydrographs, so a longer T lowers its friction multiplier.
