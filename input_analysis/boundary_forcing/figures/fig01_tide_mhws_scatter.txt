Mean high water springs (MHWS, height above local mean sea level) of three tide sources against the
GESLA tide gauges, at the 698 quality sites of the tide set (harmonic fit over >= 3 years explaining
>= 50% of the variance, GTSM station within 10 km). Each dot is one gauge; the grey line is 1:1. (a)
GTSM v3 tide-only run at the paired GTSM station; (b) FES2022 atlas at the gauge position; (c)
FES2014 atlas at the gauge position. The box gives the median of the ratio source / gauge, the mean
absolute error (MAE) and the number of sites n.

All sources go through the same chain, so method differences cannot explain what remains: GESLA and
GTSM series are fitted by least squares with the 34 FES constituents (nodal corrections, inferred
minor constituents), FES uses its atlas constants, all are predicted over the same 18.61-year nodal
cycle at 10 min without the seasonal Sa / Ssa terms, and MHWS is computed by the same code
(src/tide_statistics.py).

Why n differs between the panels: every quality site has a GTSM station, so (a) has all 698. FES
values are read at the gauge itself and need a valid atlas cell within 20 km. FES2022 has no value
at none (sites lost to a since-fixed extraction error, filled by rerunning levels_tides.py), FES2014
none at Vancouver_Bc, Point_Atkinson_BC, Campbell_River_BC (inner harbours outside its 1/16 deg
ocean mask).

Reading: GTSM's tide is 8% too large in the median, mostly from S2 and M2 (fig03). FES2022 matches
the gauges best; FES2014 is close but has more scatter, which is why FES2022 is the primary tide
source and FES2014 only the fallback at basin heads (fig04-fig06).
