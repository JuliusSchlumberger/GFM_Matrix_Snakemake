The terms that turn a storm-tide level (above local MSL) into the total level of the WL_scenarios
files, at the 22,670 stations north of 60 S: total = storm tide + MDT + 2020 offset + SLR
fingerprint (`preparation/prepare_boundary_conditions.py`). (a) The mean dynamic topography, local
MSL above the geoid of the DEM (CNES-CLS22, 1993-2012 mean). (b) The 2020 offset: AR6's global-mean
rise from 1995-2014 to 2020 (0.049 m), spread regionally with the SLR fingerprint, so that SLR 0
means the sea level of 2020 rather than of the MDT epoch. (c) The change of the RP100 level at SLR 0
between the former WL_scenarios (COAST-RP + MDT) and the current ones (storm-tide product + MDT +
2020 offset); the MDT is the same in both and cancels. (d) Distribution of the change and its two
parts.

Reading: the MDT spans -0.08 to 1.14 m (5-95%), highest in the Indo-Pacific warm pool and lowest in
the North Atlantic, the Arctic and the Southern Ocean; it is the largest datum term. The offset is
5.6 cm at the median station (0.8 to 7.2 cm): near zero or negative where land still rises after the
last glaciation (Hudson Bay, the Baltic, Alaska), above the global 4.9 cm on the US Gulf and
Atlantic coasts and in South-east Asia. The change of the WL_scenarios level is dominated by the
storm-tide product (median +4.9 cm, 5-95% -0.31 to +0.48 m, its pattern as in fig12); the offset
adds a few centimetres almost everywhere (median total +10.3 cm). The step at zero in (d) is 93
stations whose product level equals COAST-RP: the 15 GTSM fallbacks and tropical stations where
COAST-RP's TC part alone sets RP100 and therefore sets the product's RP100 too.
