Hydrographs whose maximum is not at time 0, the high water the generator's surge is centred on. (a)
Their share by return period and tidal type (form factor (K1 + O1) / (M2 + S2) of the FES tide). (b)
Where the maximum falls instead. (c) How far it exceeds the level at time 0. Every maximum equals
the RP level, which is what the SFINCS forcing and correction B use.

Reading: 4.1% of all hydrographs and 3.3% at RP100 peak elsewhere, most often at RP2 in semidiurnal
tides. In 64% the maximum is one day away (+-24.8 h), in 19% one tidal cycle (+-12.4 h), in 8%
within the hour around time 0: the window's centre high water is the median tide at the annual
maxima, but a neighbouring high water (diurnal inequality, spring-neap cycle) is higher, and the
slowly varying surge lifts it above the centre. Half of them exceed the time-0 level by less than
1.6 cm, 99% by less than 20 cm. The event stays physical: the storm's maximum lands on the highest
high water within it, and the maximum is the RP level in every case.
