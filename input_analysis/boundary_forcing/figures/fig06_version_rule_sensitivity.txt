How the two thresholds of the FES version rule were chosen. The rule replaces FES2022 by FES2014 at
a point only if both conditions hold: (1) FES2022's MHWS is more than R times FES2014's, and (2)
FES2022's MHWS exceeds FES2014's by more than D metres. R is a relative and D an absolute margin: R
alone would switch microtidal coasts where both values are a few centimetres, D alone would switch
large-tide coasts where FES2022 is only a little higher. Each cell of the two panels is one
candidate pair (R vertical, D horizontal). The red frame is the adopted pair, R = 1.5 and D = 1.25
m.

(a) What the candidate rule does at the 905 GESLA tide gauges that have MHWS from GESLA, FES2022 and
FES2014. At every gauge where the rule would switch to FES2014, the switch is scored against the
gauge's own MHWS: an improvement if FES2014 is closer to the gauge than FES2022 by more than 5 cm, a
worsening if it is further by more than 5 cm. Cell text: improvements / worsenings. Colour: the
root-mean-square error (RMSE) of MHWS against GESLA over all 905 gauges when the candidate rule is
applied, i.e. FES2014 where it fires and FES2022 elsewhere; darker = lower error. The scale spans
the pairs with R <= 1.75 (0.129-0.138 m); worse pairs (R >= 2, up to 0.235 m) are white. RMSE
squares the errors, so it is dominated by the few gross ones: FES2022 alone is 0.233 m mostly
because of Skagway (error 4.7 m), and FES2014 alone would be 0.153 m although its mean absolute
error is larger than FES2022's (fig01). It therefore measures exactly what the rule is for: removing
gross errors without adding new ones. The gauges with a real FES2022 error (Skagway, Juneau, Port
Alberni) are the improvements to be had; Trondheim, where FES2014 is wrong, is the main worsening to
avoid. Dotted squares: pairs with no worsening and the most improvements (3).

(b) What the candidate rule does at 178 known artefacts: boundary nodes in the four basins of fig05
whose FES2022 MHWS exceeds 1.25 x the largest MHWS observed in that basin (Minas 8.15 m, Strait of
Magellan 5.5 m, Colorado mouth 5.0 m, Lynn Canal 2.8 m). These nodes are wrong whatever FES2014
says, so they test the rule without circularity. Cell text: how many of the 178 the rule replaces;
colour: that share (white 0%, dark blue 100%). Dotted squares: pairs that replace all of them. In
this range of D the result does not depend on D, because the artefacts exceed FES2014 by several
metres: only R decides.

(c) How many of the 102,935 FES boundary nodes of the test tiles the candidate rule switches to
FES2014 (cell text; colour on a logarithmic scale, darker = more). At the adopted pair this is 1,287
nodes, the red crosses of fig04. Pairs that switch more nodes than needed change FES2022 values on
coasts where no evidence says it is wrong.

Reading off the adopted values: (a) leaves the dotted pairs in the column D = 1.25 m (lowest RMSE,
0.129 m; no worsening, all 3 improvements; at D <= 1 m Trondheim is switched wrongly, at D = 1.5 m
Port Alberni is no longer caught). (b) leaves R <= 1.5 (higher R misses artefacts). The row R = 1
switches the ratio condition off, so only D decides: from R = 1 to R = 1.5 panels (a) and (b) are
identical, i.e. the ratio changes nothing at the gauges or at the known artefacts. It only changes
how many other nodes are switched (c: 1,464 at R = 1, 1,287 at R = 1.5, for D = 1.25 m). R is
therefore a precaution for coasts without evidence, not a value the data pin down; R = 1.5 is the
largest ratio that still replaces every known artefact. At the adopted pair: 3 improvements, 0
worsenings, 178 of 178 artefacts replaced.

Behind the D bound: comparing the two atlases at all 905 gauges, binned by how much FES2022 exceeds
FES2014 in MHWS (gain = |FES2022 - GESLA| - |FES2014 - GESLA|, positive when FES2014 is better):
-inf .. -0.25 m: n=29, FES2014 closer at 31%, median gain -0.34 m; -0.25 .. -0.10 m: n=32, FES2014
closer at 44%, median gain -0.04 m; -0.10 .. +0.10 m: n=777, FES2014 closer at 41%, median gain
-0.00 m; +0.10 .. +0.25 m: n=44, FES2014 closer at 43%, median gain -0.03 m; +0.25 .. +0.50 m: n=22,
FES2014 closer at 50%, median gain +0.02 m; +0.50 .. +0.75 m: n=3, FES2014 closer at 0%, median gain
-0.64 m; +1.00 .. +inf m: n=5, FES2014 closer at 80%, median gain +1.43 m. Below about 1 m FES2014
is not the better atlas, so switching there does harm. The gauge evidence rests on few sites (5 with
a difference above 1 m), so the known artefacts carry the ratio bound.
