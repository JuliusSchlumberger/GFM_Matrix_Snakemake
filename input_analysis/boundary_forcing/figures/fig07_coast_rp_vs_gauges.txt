COAST-RP storm-tide return levels against the tide gauges, by IPCC AR6 reference region (Iturbide et
al., 2020). Sites: the record-based extremes set (623 GESLA sites with >= 20 complete years since
1979 and a GTSM station within 10 km, whatever their tide). Per site the difference is COAST-RP at
the paired station minus a GEV fit to the gauge's annual maxima (levels relative to local mean sea
level), at (a) RP10 and (b) RP100. Dot = median over the region's sites, bar = interquartile range;
negative = COAST-RP too low. (c) Number of sites per region (log scale). The top row pools all
sites.

Regions: each gauge takes the nearest AR6 land region within 200 km, otherwise the AR6 ocean region
containing it (remote islands, e.g. Hawaii in the North Pacific Ocean region). Regions with fewer
than 5 sites are pooled in the last row. Regions are ordered by their RP100 median, most negative
first. The numbers are in fig07_coast_rp_vs_gauges_data.csv.

Reading: overall COAST-RP is -0.06 m (RP10) and -0.07 m (RP100) relative to the gauges. That near-
zero median hides two offsetting errors: GTSM's tide is too large (fig01) and its surge too low, so
with the tide excess removed COAST-RP is about 0.11-0.12 m too low. The largest deficits are in
tropical-cyclone regions, where 30-40-year gauge records also under-sample cyclones, so the gauge
GEV is itself uncertain there.
