COAST-RP storm-tide return levels against the tide gauges, by IPCC AR6 reference region (Iturbide et
al., 2020). Both sides are total still-water levels (tide plus surge) relative to local mean sea
level: COAST-RP at the paired GTSM station, and a GEV fit to the gauge's annual maxima. Sites: the
record-based extremes set (623 GESLA sites with >= 20 complete years since 1979 and a GTSM station
within 10 km, whatever their tide).

(a) COAST-RP minus gauge per region: dot = median over the region's sites, bar = interquartile
range; blue = RP10, orange = RP100. Negative = COAST-RP too low. (b) Number of sites per region (log
scale). The top row pools all sites.

Order: regions are grouped by their AR6 continent in a fixed geographic order (North, Central and
South America, Europe, the Mediterranean, Africa, Asia, Oceania, the polar land regions, then the
ocean regions of remote islands: Atlantic, Indian, Pacific, Arctic, Southern), alphabetically within
each group; the italic labels name the groups. Each gauge takes the nearest AR6 land region within
200 km, otherwise the AR6 ocean region containing it. Regions with fewer than 5 sites are pooled in
the last row. The numbers are in fig07_coast_rp_vs_gauges_data.csv.

Reading: overall COAST-RP is -0.06 m (RP10) and -0.07 m (RP100) relative to the gauges. That near-
zero median hides two offsetting errors: GTSM's tide is too large (fig01) and its surge too low, so
with the tide excess removed COAST-RP is about 0.11-0.12 m too low. COAST-RP is lowest in tropical-
cyclone regions (eastern North America, East Asia, the Caribbean, the Pacific), where 30-40-year
gauge records also under-sample cyclones, and too high in West and Central Europe, where GTSM's tide
excess dominates.
