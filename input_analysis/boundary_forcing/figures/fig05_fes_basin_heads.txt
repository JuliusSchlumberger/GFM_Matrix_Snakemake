MHWS (mean high water springs above local mean sea level) at the FES boundary nodes in the four
basins where FES2022 has gross artefacts. Each grey frame is one basin and holds two maps of the
same area: FES2022 on top, FES2014 below, at the same nodes. Circles: nodes that keep FES2022 under
the version rule; crosses: nodes where the rule replaces FES2022 by FES2014 (fig04, fig06).
Triangles: GESLA tide gauges, coloured by their observed MHWS. Both maps of a frame share the colour
scale below them. Each map is cropped to its nodes and gauges and drawn with the east-west scale
corrected for latitude, so shapes are true. Land: Natural Earth 1:50m, grey.

(a, b) Minas Basin / Cobequid Bay: 149 nodes, 34 replaced; FES2022 MHWS max 14.5 m, FES2014 max 7.2
m; no gauge (c, d) Strait of Magellan / Bahia Lomas: 163 nodes, 48 replaced; FES2022 MHWS max 16.3
m, FES2014 max 4.6 m; no gauge (e, f) Upper Gulf of California: 114 nodes, 1 replaced; FES2022 MHWS
max 15.4 m, FES2014 max 3.0 m; no gauge (g, h) Glacier Bay / Lynn Canal: 217 nodes, 106 replaced;
FES2022 MHWS max 12.2 m, FES2014 max 1.8 m; gauges: Juneau 2.72 m, Skagway 2.78 m

Observed references without a gauge in the frame: Minas Basin mean spring range 14.5 m, record 16.3
m (MHWS about 7.3 m; Desplanque and Mossman, 2001); Strait of Magellan spring range about 9 m on the
Atlantic side (Medeiros and Kjerfve, 1988); Colorado mouth spring range 7-10 m (Archer, 2013).

Reading: FES2022 is plausible over most of each basin but gives MHWS as large as the whole observed
spring range at a few head nodes; FES2014 shows none of these hot spots and stays within the
observed ranges. The rule replaces exactly those head nodes.
