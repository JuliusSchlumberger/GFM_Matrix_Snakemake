MHWS (mean high water springs above local mean sea level) at the FES boundary nodes in the four
basins where FES2022 has gross artefacts: (a) Minas Basin / Cobequid Bay, (b) Strait of Magellan /
Bahia Lomas, (c) upper Gulf of California, (d) Glacier Bay / Lynn Canal. For each basin the map
marked 1 shows FES2022 and the map marked 2 below it FES2014, at the same nodes and over the same
area. All maps share the colour scale at the bottom.

Symbols: circles = nodes that keep FES2022 under the version rule (fig04, fig06). Crosses (maps 1) =
FES2022 values the rule discards; diamonds (maps 2) = the FES2014 values that replace them. Red
ovals enclose the replaced nodes: nodes closer than about three grid cells form one group, and each
oval is the smallest axis-aligned ellipse around the box spanned by the group's westernmost,
easternmost, northernmost and southernmost node. Stars = GESLA tide gauges, coloured by their
observed MHWS. Each map is cropped to its nodes and gauges and drawn with the east-west scale
corrected for latitude, so shapes are true. Land: Natural Earth 1:50m, grey.

Observed references without a gauge in the map: Minas Basin mean spring range 14.5 m, record 16.3 m
(MHWS about 7.3 m; Desplanque and Mossman, 2001); Strait of Magellan spring range about 9 m on the
Atlantic side (Medeiros and Kjerfve, 1988); Colorado mouth spring range 7-10 m (Archer, 2013).

Reading: FES2022 is plausible over most of each basin but gives MHWS as large as the whole observed
spring range at a few head nodes; FES2014 shows none of these hot spots and stays within the
observed ranges. The rule replaces exactly those head nodes.
