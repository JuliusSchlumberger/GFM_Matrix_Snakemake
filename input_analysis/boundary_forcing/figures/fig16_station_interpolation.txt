How well the level at a coastline cell can be interpolated from the stations around it, tested by
leaving each station out and predicting its RP100 level (new product) from the others (22,670
stations north of 60 S). (a) Mean absolute error of inverse-distance weighting over the k nearest
stations (haversine distance, floored at 1 km) for three weighting powers, and of the nearest
station alone (dashed). (b) The error by the distance from the left-out station to its nearest
neighbour, for the nearest station, k = 5 and production's k = 15 (both power 2). Production also
restricts the neighbours to the tile's ocean component, which this global test does not.

Reading: fewer neighbours or a higher power interpolate the level field better than production's k =
15, power 2, but the gain is about 1.5 cm of MAE (6.9 cm at production, 5.4 cm at best, 6.9 cm for
the nearest station). The error is set by station spacing: 2 cm where stations are within 5 km, 8-12
cm at 10-50 km and about 20 cm beyond 50 km.
