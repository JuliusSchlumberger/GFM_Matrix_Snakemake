The MHWS ratios of fig01 as maps (Equal Earth projection; land in grey). Each dot is one quality
tide gauge; its colour is the ratio of the source's MHWS to the gauge's MHWS on a logarithmic scale
centred on 1: red = source too large, blue = too small, white = equal. Ratios beyond 0.67 or 1.5
take the end colours. Dots closest to 1 are drawn first so that departures stay visible where gauges
cluster. (a) GTSM v3 tide; (b) FES2022.

Reading: GTSM is too large almost everywhere (Africa and the Mediterranean ~20%, NW Europe ~10%) and
too small on the North American Atlantic and Gulf coast. FES2022 is close to 1 nearly everywhere;
its remaining departures sit in estuaries, fjords and narrow entrances (upper St Lawrence, Long
Island Sound, the Salish Sea, Port Phillip entrance, Juneau). FES2022 read at the GTSM station
instead of the gauge gives the same values (MAE 0.01 m), so the GTSM pattern is a model bias, not a
location effect.
