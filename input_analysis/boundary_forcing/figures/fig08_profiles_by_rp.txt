Median storm-tide profile of the annual maxima, by empirical return period (RP), at the quality tide
gauges. Every event is cut out of the record with +-72 h around its peak (+-48 h shown), expressed
relative to local mean sea level (horizontal line at 0) and divided by its peak, so each event
starts at 1 at hour 0. Each line is the median over all events of a class at every hour. Rows: (a,
b) GESLA gauge records, (c, d) GTSM (surge + harmonic tide) at the paired station, 1979-2024.
Columns: gauges with a spring tidal range of 0-1 m (a, c) and 2-4 m (b, d). Blue shades: RP classes
from light (RP 0-2 y) to dark (RP 20-50 y). Dashed green: the COAST-HG spring-tide RP100 hydrograph
at the paired stations (201 / 159 sites), the shape the pipeline uses for every RP.

Empirical RP: each event's rank among its site's annual maxima, RP = (n + 1) / rank with n the
number of complete years. With 30-46 years of record the largest RP is about 45, so the top class
holds RP 20-~45 events. GESLA events per class (0-1 m / 2-4 m range): RP 0-2 y: 3117 / 2438; RP 2-5
y: 1867 / 1455; RP 5-10 y: 626 / 492; RP 10-20 y: 300 / 238; RP 20-50 y: 225 / 170.

Why the curves look so smooth and aligned: the peak sits at hour 0 and equals 1 by construction, the
median over hundreds to thousands of events removes the individual noise, and the semidiurnal tide
(period 12.42 h) puts the neighbouring high waters of nearly every event at the same hours. The
event-to-event spread around these medians is considerable but not drawn.

Reading: rarer events are more surge-dominated, so their neighbouring high waters are lower relative
to the peak, most clearly in the microtidal class (a, c), where the next-day high water falls from
about 0.8 to 0.55 of the peak. On microtidal coasts COAST-HG's next-day high water lies within the
observed range, but between the high waters it drains to about -0.3 of the peak where observed
events stay near mean sea level. On meso- and macrotidal coasts (b, d) COAST-HG's neighbouring high
waters are lower than every observed class. Both mean a shorter time near the peak, i.e. a shorter T
for the eikonal duration correction. Test 7 (fig09) traces the meso- and macrotidal deficit to
COAST-HG's tide window, not to its surge.
