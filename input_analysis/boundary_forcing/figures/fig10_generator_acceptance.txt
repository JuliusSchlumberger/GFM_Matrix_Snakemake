Acceptance test of the per-RP storm-tide hydrograph generator against the tide gauges
(boundary_test/generator_validation.py). For every quality site (tide set) hydrographs are built at
the paired GTSM station for RP 2, 10, 25, 50 and 100 and compared with the gauge's own annual maxima
of the matching empirical return period (within a factor 2 of the target RP; for RP 50 and 100,
beyond the 30-46-year records, the gauge's events of RP 20-45). Columns: gauges with a spring tidal
range of (a, e) 0-1 m, (b, f) 1-2 m, (c, g) 2-4 m, (d, h) more than 4 m (201, 259, 159, 79 sites).
Top row: T(0.25), the hours the water level stays within a quarter of the peak (above mean sea
level), the duration of the eikonal correction; bottom row: the next-day high water as a fraction of
the peak. Each point is the median over the sites of a class.

Black: the gauges, with the grey band of +-15% that the acceptance test allows. Blue solid: the
generator as decided (FES2022 tide around a high water at the station's own tide level during
extremes; surge shape = median normalised GTSM surge of the station's events of that RP window,
smoothed by a 13-h running mean that removes the tidal-period ripple of the non-tidal residual;
scaled to COAST-RP). Blue dotted: the same without smoothing. Orange: the HGRAPHER port (spring high
water nearest MHWS, shape from events of matching surge size). Green diamond: COAST-HG at RP100. At
tropical-cyclone RPs the surge shape comes from another quality gauge within 50 km, otherwise a
Gaussian; a gauge is never scored with its own shape.

Cells (tidal class x RP) within +-15% on all three measures (T(0.25), T(0.5), next-day high water):
smoothed generator 14 of 20, raw generator 10 of 20, HGRAPHER 0 of 20, COAST-HG 0 of 4. Data:
generator_acceptance.csv and generator_scores.parquet.

Reading: the generator fixes COAST-HG's main error. Its next-day high waters match the gauges in
every class, and T(0.25) rises from COAST-HG's 5-6 h to within 10-20% of the gauges, falling with RP
as observed. The remaining misses are T(0.25) at RP 2 on microtidal and macrotidal coasts (about 30%
too short) and the RP 100 point, which is compared with RP 20-45 gauge events because nothing rarer
is observed. Scaling to the gauge events' own peaks instead of COAST-RP leaves the RP 2 deficit
unchanged, so it comes from the shape, not from COAST-RP.
