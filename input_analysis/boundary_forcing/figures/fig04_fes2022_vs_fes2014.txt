FES2022 against FES2014 at the 102,935 FES boundary nodes of the test tiles (the atlas grid nodes
that seed the tiles' boundary cells). At every node both atlases are predicted over the same
18.61-year nodal cycle at 10 min and reduced to MHWS (mean high water springs, height above local
mean sea level) exactly as at the tide gauges (fig01).

(a) Map (Equal Earth; land grey) of the ratio MHWS_FES2022 / MHWS_FES2014 per node on a logarithmic
scale centred on 1: red = FES2022 higher, blue = FES2014 higher; beyond 1/3 and 3 the end colours.
Nodes closest to 1 are drawn first. (b) The same nodes as a scatter, FES2014 on x, FES2022 on y;
thin line 1:1. Dashed line: the boundary of the version rule. A node above it, i.e. with
MHWS_FES2022 > 1.5 x MHWS_FES2014 and MHWS_FES2022 - MHWS_FES2014 > 1.25 m, takes FES2014 instead of
FES2022 (red crosses, 1,287 nodes = 1.3%); grey dots keep FES2022. How R = 1.5 and D = 1.25 m were
chosen is shown in fig06.

Reading: at almost all nodes the two FES versions agree (ratio within 0.9-1.1). The outliers where
FES2022 is far higher are a few basin and fjord heads (fig05). The ratio alone also flags microtidal
coasts where both MHWS are a few centimetres; the minimum difference D keeps those on FES2022.
